1. One did not need a fancy degree or even formal education to succeed in the stock market.
    1. With a bit of skill, perseverance and, most importantly, luck, there was no limit to the money one could make trading in shares.
2. There are plenty of rags-to-riches stories on Dalal Street that get talked about often.
    1. What doesn’t get publicised as much are the riches-to-rags stories, which far outnumber the success stories.
    2. People get carried away, thinking it is easy to make money in the stock market, and learn the truth the hardway.
3. **Short selling** means selling the shares without owning them, in the hope that shares price will fall and that you will be able to buy them back cheap.
4. No matter how big or accomplished a player, nobody can correctly predict the top or bottom of a stock
5. There may be plenty of fundamentally sound companies. But you can’t make big money unless you are able to buy them cheap.
6. Higher rate means. company has to pay more on their loans.
    1. This squeezes their profit margin
    2. This will cause stock prices to fall in anticipation of reduced corporate earnings
7. When people can earn 12 per cent on their fixed deposits without breaking a sweat, why would they lose sleep to make 20-25 per cent from equities (where the risk is much higher?)
8. It is only when everybody tells you to buy a stock or sector that you should have second thoughts about them.
9. When stocks became ovrevalues and people tried to justify it rather than admit they were expensive, it is time to book profits.
10. Once overconfidence creeps in, it creates room for indiscipline, and bigger errors of judgement follow
11. In January 2002, Bharti Tele-ventures announced its IPO, the 1st 100 per cent book-built issue in the history of Indian capital market
    1. In a book-built issue, the price is discovered by gauging investor demand for the shares.
    2. This is different from fixed price IPO, where the company offers shares to the public at a price decided by the company
12. The easier it becomes to make money off the shares, the closer you are to the day of reckoning
13. Strong Capital flows was a good thing, but excess of it spelt trouble as it would make the Rupee too strong
    1. By extension, Indian exports uncompetitive
    
14. One sure sign of trouble is when businessman and traders outside the stock market start heading to the market, convinced this is the place to make good money
    1. The problem is they ignore their main business and start chasing easy money
15. Bull market has no top; bear market has no bottoms
16. Never let yur ego hurt your judgement
17. Conventional market wisom has it that a blockbuster IPO usually signals the peak of bull run
18. About Stock Splits: Ten notes of ₹100 and one note of ₹1000 are same
19. A promoter buying a stock shows his confidence in the prospects for his company. 
    1. But, he may be taking a two-year view, are you willing to take similar view?
20. If an investor goes around talking up the stock, it means that he has bought entire quantity that he was looking to buy
21. A long period of underperformance usually precedes removal from Sensex or Nifty.
22. Value Investors are not happy if a company raises equity capital often, even if new investors are willing to pay premium.
23. If broker knows the results are going to be good, many others too will know.
24. Business too have bad phase, like us.
    1. But if business model is good and promoters intentions are fair, it will overcome the problem before long
    2. You can’t write off a company for one setback; just as you can’t sack an employee for a single offence
